Architecture figures for PyTorch, a murderboard, an AI coding short course, and research tools.

I build small, open-source tools that report their own failure modes as loudly as their results. draughtsman draws a PyTorch model's architecture and then proves mechanically that nothing was dropped on the way to the picture. The research tools — for measuring things in neuroscience data, and for knowing when the measurement fails — run in your browser: no install, no account, and your data never leaves your computer. The rest are not instruments — a review process for any document that makes claims, tied to no field and improved by being run against itself, and a short course on working with coding agents. They exist because plausible and correct are not the same thing.

What these have in common

A tool that flatters itself is worse than no tool.

They all grew out of one problem: an instrument always returns an answer, and that answer is easiest to trust when nobody has checked what it cost. So each reports its failure modes as loudly as its results. The murderboard does the same for documents, and the short course for the agent that helped write them.

  • Nothing uploaded No backend, no accounts, no analytics, no cookies, on all five sites. In the research tools the algorithm ships inside the page and runs on your own device, so a recording you analyse is never transmitted.
  • Validated, not asserted Ports are diffed point-by-point against the implementation they claim to reproduce; detectors are scored against known ground truth, not against each other.
  • Open source All six repositories are public, caveats and references included.
  • Readable without a browser Each site carries a static, JavaScript-free account of what its tool does; each research-tool repository carries an instruction file written for an agent rather than a person.
